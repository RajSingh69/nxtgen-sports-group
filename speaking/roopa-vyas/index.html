Who is Roopa Vyas?

She works where culture is felt, not just written down.

Roopa Vyas is an Equality, Diversity and Inclusion professional at Transport for Wales, Chief Operating Officer of Her Game Too, football advocate, speaker, host, trainer and content creator.

Her work connects professional EDI practice, women in football, Welsh identity, South Asian heritage, supporter culture, media, community and representation.

Liverpool context

From supporter to change-maker.

Roopa's connection to Liverpool is rooted in family, supporter identity and community. Through Her Game Too partnership work, that connection has extended into practical supporter inclusion: education, reporting, signposting, fan forums, frontline staff conversations and matchday environments.

First Time Fans

What does inclusion look like?

Sometimes it looks like a woman or girl walking into a football stadium for the first time and knowing where to go, who to ask and that the space is for her too.

This is the difference between saying everyone is welcome and making someone actually feel welcome.

What your organisation can learn

What can Roopa bring to your room?

01

Inclusion that people can feel

Real-world context: Values and policies can exist without creating confidence.

Roopa's experience: Working across EDI systems, networks and football spaces where belonging is tested publicly.

Question: Does your lived culture match your stated values?

02

Challenging normalised behaviour

Real-world context: Sexism, exclusion and poor behaviour can become accepted through silence.

Roopa's experience: Her Game Too work around education, reporting and practical challenge.

Question: What behaviour have you normalised?

03

Creating belonging

Real-world context: Being invited in is not the same as feeling safe to participate.

Roopa's experience: Women in football, supporter culture, Wales, identity and community.

Question: Who feels they have to change themselves to belong?

04

Representation with purpose

Real-world context: Visibility matters, but it needs voice, access and accountability.

Roopa's experience: Public work around women, South Asian identity, football and media.

Question: Who is represented before decisions are made?

05

Having difficult conversations

Real-world context: Inclusion work involves behaviour, discomfort, identity and power.

Roopa's experience: Speaking, hosting, content and facilitation across complex subjects.

Question: How do you make difficult conversations useful enough to matter?

What should people leave thinking about?

  • Does our lived culture match our stated values?
  • Who feels comfortable speaking up?
  • Who feels represented?
  • Who has to edit themselves to belong?
  • Are people invited in before decisions are made?
  • What does practical allyship look like?
